# Project brief: Corridor (working title)

> **Audience:** This document is the build brief for a coding agent. Build the project described here from scratch. Nothing is assumed to exist yet.
>  **Document rule:** Do not edit this file unless the project owner explicitly asks. Put implementation notes and agent instructions in `AGENTS.md`.

## 1. Project identity

- **Name:** Undecided. "Corridor" is the working title.
- **Purpose:** Hackathon project for the theme "Create breakthrough ideas and futuristic technologies that could transform the fight against substance abuse."
- **Hackathon date:** 28 November 2026.
- **Geographic focus:** Kerala, India, including inbound, outbound and transit connections beyond Kerala when supported by data.
- **Primary product:** A forecasting model for drug logistics and movements, supported by a geographic analyst workspace.
- **Context:** Inspired by Kerala's Operation Toofan. This is a team proposal. It is not an official agency system, an Operation Toofan deployment, or a confirmed agency partnership. Do not use branding that implies official status.

## 2. Primary goal

**Build a forecasting model that learns historical drug logistics, movements and seizures across Kerala, combines those patterns with recent verified signals, and forecasts likely future movement areas, corridors and time windows where authorized enforcement agencies may have opportunities to intercept drugs. Forecast quality must be measured against later outcomes.**

Central questions the product must help answer:

1. Where and when might drug movement or related activity occur next?
2. Which origins, transit connections, destinations and transport patterns could be involved?
3. Which areas or connections warrant an agency's interception review, given the forecast, evidence, observation coverage and uncertainty?
4. How accurately did previous forecasts anticipate independently documented outcomes, and did they beat simple recent-history methods?

**The forecasting model is the core.** The map presents its geographic outputs, evidence tools make predictions inspectable, ingestion and news collection supply reviewed inputs, and briefs communicate results. Prevention, recovery and community features are supporting capabilities. When trade-offs arise, prefer whatever improves forecasting data, learning, evaluation, timeliness or analyst usefulness.

The first model targets documented-case counts (section 5). That is a starting experiment toward movement and interception forecasting, not the final definition of the product.

## 3. Users and workflow

**Primary users:** authorized anti-narcotics analysts and enforcement personnel, initially district level in Kerala Police or Excise. They review forecasts alongside their own intelligence. **Secondary users:** prevention coordinators and authorized health or rehabilitation planners, who use suitable aggregate findings.

Intended workflow the system supports:

1. Obtain authorized historical seizure, movement and logistics records with source dates and reporting coverage.
2. Normalize and deduplicate cases; review locations, movement claims, quantities, transport modes and unknowns.
3. Build geographic and temporal features, train candidate models, and compare them with simple baselines on later unseen periods.
4. Collect recent case updates, permitted news and other cues. Preserve when each signal became available and verify it before model use.
5. Generate forecasts for defined future areas, connections and time windows, with uncertainty, evidence context and a data-support assessment.
6. Let an analyst inspect a forecast on the map, compare it with historical observations, and prepare an agency-review brief about a possible interception opportunity.
7. Record later documented outcomes, coverage and enforcement effort; measure forecast errors; use reviewed updates to improve the next model version.
8. Share appropriate aggregate findings with prevention or recovery planners where their separate local evidence supports it.

No live agency data access, integration or reporting arrangement exists. Real-world evaluation and use would require agency authorization and participation.

## 4. Forecast definition

A forecast record must identify:

- Prediction date, information cutoff, and future time window.
- The area, or directed origin/destination corridor, plus transport or transit context where supported.
- The exact target estimated (for example future documented cases, a documented movement event, or an interception outcome under a defined observation regime).
- The estimate: a count, ranking, or probability appropriate to the target. Probabilities require validation and calibration. A ranking score is not a probability.
- An uncertainty range, or an explicit statement that uncertainty cannot yet be established.
- Supporting historical patterns, recent verified cues, source availability, coverage gaps, and model version.
- Whether the model has enough support to forecast or should **abstain**.

Rules:

- Geographic scale and horizon must match the evidence. District-level records support district-level claims only.
- Candidate connections proposed by a model remain hypotheses until independently supported. The existence of a road or transport link does not show that drugs moved along it.
- New-corridor prediction requires its own evaluation. Do not borrow performance claims from known corridors.

## 5. Models to build

### Initial targets (build first)

Over **7-day and 28-day** horizons:

1. **Area model:** future documented seizure-case counts in a defined area.
2. **Corridor model:** future documented case counts involving an already-known origin/destination connection.

These do not measure all trafficking or the probability of successful interception, and they do not discover unknown corridors or reconstruct journeys. State this wherever results are shown.

### Algorithms

- **Baselines (required):** last-N-days average over covered history, seasonal naive, and same-period-last-year.
- **Candidate:** Poisson histogram gradient boosting (scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingRegressor` with `loss="poisson"`), compared with the baselines.
- Produce versioned model files, evaluation files, and aggregate JSON and HTML reports.
- Add more complex models (graph or network features, finer geography, shorter horizons) only when held-out performance justifies them.

### Progression

As richer movement-level records become available, extend toward movement and interception targets, selected by data quality and held-out results. Do not treat the count model as a permanent ceiling.

## 6. Data

### Canonical case schema

One row per case, not per article. Fields:

- `case_id`, `source_ref`, responsible `agency`, `evidence_status`, independent corroboration.
- `event_date`, `first_available_date`, `verified_date`, withdrawal/correction history.
- Seizure location, documented origin, destination and transit points, with geographic precision and uncertainty.
- Substance category, quantity, explicit measurement unit.
- Transport mode, and links between cases.
- Reporting completeness by area/connection and period; aggregate enforcement or observation effort where available.
- Source permissions, coverage definitions, revisions, extraction dates.

### Data rules

- Missing locations, endpoints and coverage are **unknown, not zero**. Never silently convert them to facts.
- Several reports of one case are one event. Deduplicate repeated and syndicated reports.
- Do not expand annual totals or public statistics into invented case records, and do not treat them as proof of routes.
- Keep seizure-only observations separate from documented-route records.
- Do not claim to hold every past case; claims apply to the authorized reporting universe.

### Synthetic data (required for build and demo)

No real records are available to the agent. Build a **synthetic data generator** that produces realistic Kerala case data in the canonical schema, including districts, known corridors, seasonality, reporting delays, uneven coverage and missing fields. Requirements:

- Label all synthetic data and every output derived from it as synthetic, in files, API responses and the UI.
- Make the pipeline data-source agnostic so real authorized data can be loaded in the same schema later.
- Results on synthetic data demonstrate implementation behavior only. They do not establish real-world accuracy, operational readiness, or reduced harm.

### Reference sources (context only; no data is assumed available)

- Kerala Police NDPS/special-law case statistics (aggregate context).
- UNODC individual drug seizure data and reporting guidelines (schema and limitation guidance).
- Public Kerala geography (administrative boundaries, roads, rail) from open sources such as OpenStreetMap, with attribution.

## 7. Recent and real-time cues

"Real-time" means an explicit ingestion and forecast-refresh process with visible latency and freshness. It does not imply a live agency feed.

- Build an RSS/Atom collector that periodically collects item **metadata** into a review queue. No feed is enabled by default. The collector never retrains the model automatically.
- Store event date, publication date, first-seen time, verification time and source identity as separate fields.
- Detect duplicates and syndicated copies. A headline or repeated allegation is not independent confirmation.
- Unverified items stay in the review queue and never alter training labels or forecasts.
- Only analyst-verified items become cue features, and only from the time they were verified.
- An LLM may later assist extraction. Its output must carry source references and be human-verified. Do not build it in the first pass.
- Aggregate contextual features (transport activity, consumption surveys, enforcement effort, environmental conditions) may be added when available at prediction time and their value is tested. Consumption data is an aggregate context measure, not an individual suspicion score.

## 8. Application features

Build the following. Keep the detail in priority order (section 12).

### Geographic map and search

- A detailed, navigable Kerala map with surrounding regional connections, scroll/touch zoom, geographic search and place profiles.
- Distinguishable layers for historical seizures, documented movements and model forecasts.
- Filters for time, geography, substance, evidence status and source, and a historical timeline.
- Search roads, addresses, destinations and landmarks using public geographic sources, with attribution and explicit unknowns.
- Forecast overlays showing predicted areas, connections and time windows. A detailed basemap must not imply that a district-level observation or forecast identifies a verified street journey.
- Predominantly black interface; use color only for meaningful transport, evidence and forecast signals.

### Evidence trail and analyst review

- Clickable sources, dates, geographic precision, documentation status, qualitative evidence assessment and review history behind every mapped observation.
- Each forecast exposes model version, information cutoff, validation results, supporting patterns and limitations.
- Keep evidence quality distinct from model uncertainty. An unvalidated confidence score must not be presented as a measured probability.
- Include an analyst/planner role switch as a **demonstration only**, labelled as not a production authorization system.

### News and community intelligence

- A news review inbox with duplicate checks and source provenance.
- A private community-lead workflow with optional contact details and human review; public intake needs no account, subject to the privacy design.
- Raw community reports never enter model training, including through automatic status changes. A case independently established must enter through the structured, reviewed evidence pipeline.
- No public feed of allegations, no automatic reports to authorities, and no promise of anonymity beyond what is actually implemented.
- Optional: explore cooperation with existing reporting channels such as Yodhav. No integration is assumed.

### Briefs, prevention and recovery

- Editable, role-appropriate planning briefs and aggregate exports. The primary use is to communicate a reviewed movement/interception hypothesis, target period, supporting evidence and uncertainty to responsible agency personnel.
- Prevention, helpline, outreach and rehabilitation planning are secondary uses of appropriate aggregate findings and require separate local evidence about need and capacity. A forecast near a school or service does not establish activity there or identify people needing care.
- The platform supports human decisions. It never automatically allocates resources or authorizes enforcement action.

### Model report view

Let the website load and inspect a local aggregate training/evaluation report (baseline comparison, errors, misses, abstentions) without uploading source records or the executable model.

## 9. Technical stack

Organize the code around the forecasting lifecycle:

1. **Ingest:** authorized historical records and timely source updates, with provenance and review queues.
2. **Normalize:** canonical cases, consistent geography/time/units, movement relationships, coverage, explicit unknowns.
3. **Build features:** historical timing, substance/quantity patterns, geographic relationships, logistics context, coverage, and verified cues available by the prediction cutoff.
4. **Train and evaluate:** baselines and candidates, chronological validation, reporting-delay handling, uncertainty estimation, reproducible versions.
5. **Forecast and refresh:** defined targets, supported resolution, freshness checks, abstention.
6. **Inspect and act through human review:** map, evidence, ranked outputs, agency-review briefs.
7. **Measure and improve:** later outcomes, errors, coverage shifts, monitoring, reviewed retraining.

Stack:

- **Website:** React + TypeScript + Leaflet.
- **API:** Cloudflare Worker with D1 storage.
- **ML pipeline:** local Python (pandas, scikit-learn), run as separate batch scripts.
- **Inference:** a **batch forecast job** that reads the model and verified cues and writes a timestamped forecasts file. The API serves it and the UI shows a freshness label. Do not build a live inference service.
- Suggested layout: `web/`, `worker/`, `ml/` (with `data/`, `features/`, `train/`, `evaluate/`, `forecast/`, `docs/`), `AGENTS.md`, `README.md`.
- Change the stack only when reliable data and inference needs justify it. Complex models must earn their place through demonstrated value.

## 10. Validation and evaluation

"Accurate" must refer to a defined target, geographic resolution, forecast horizon and outcome dataset. Do not promise a fixed accuracy percentage.

- Use only information available at each historical prediction date. Separate training, method selection, uncertainty calibration and final evaluation **chronologically** (rolling-origin splits, no random shuffling), accounting for reporting and verification delays using `first_available_date`.
- Preserve a final held-out period and evaluate on it once.
- **Counts:** measure error (MAE, Poisson deviance) against later documented counts.
- **Rankings:** measure how many later documented events fall inside the selected areas/connections, how many selected predictions were supported, and what was missed. State the number and geographic extent selected.
- **Movement/interception targets (future):** evaluate against independently documented outcomes and observation effort. A failed search, an unobserved location and a confirmed absence are different outcomes.
- Also report lead time, uncertainty coverage, performance by area, improvement over baselines, false positives, missed events, and coverage differences. Report synthetic and real-data performance separately.
- Evaluate new locations/connections separately wherever generalization is claimed.
- Report places where the model loses to a baseline.
- Implement **abstention** when history or coverage is too thin.
- Seizure records reflect both underlying activity and enforcement/reporting. A good seizure-count test does not establish accurate prediction of all movements or successful interception. Prospective agency-led evaluation is needed before operational claims.

## 11. Data handling, safeguards and limitations

- Forecast at aggregate place, area or corridor level. Do not use personal names, IDs, gender, individual consumer records or live GPS tracking. Do not create individual suspicion scores or demographic proxies for criminality.
- Treat missing or incomplete reporting as unknown. Audit differences in enforcement effort, source availability, coverage and errors across areas, including feedback loops from acting on earlier forecasts. Show coverage gaps in the UI so a blank area reads as "unknown", not "safe".
- Preserve provenance, model/input versions and review history.
- Keep real source records out of any public demonstration. Aggregate outputs need disclosure review; a minimum-group threshold alone does not guarantee anonymity.
- Forecasts are decision support, not proof of wrongdoing or a sole basis for searches, arrests or enforcement. Distinguish observations, unverified claims and model hypotheses throughout the product.
- Show a persistent notice: "Proposed by the team. Not an official agency system. Decision support only."

## 13. Demonstration

The demo must tell one story: **historical data → reviewed recent cues → learned patterns → future geographic forecast → measured accuracy and uncertainty → agency review of a possible interception opportunity.** Supporting screens exist to make that story understandable and inspectable.

Be transparent in the demo that results come from synthetic data, that the model estimates documented cases rather than all movement, and that agency-led prospective evaluation is the next step before any operational use.

## 14. Questions the team must be able to answer

- **What are you building?** A Kerala-focused forecasting model that learns historical drug logistics, movement and seizure patterns and uses verified recent cues to forecast possible future movements and interception opportunities for agency review.
- **What makes it useful?** Timely, geographically explicit forecasts with measurable performance, inspectable evidence, uncertainty, and comparison with what simple recent-history methods would predict.
- **What is implemented?** A trainable, evaluated synthetic-data pipeline for future documented area/corridor case counts, plus the geographic, evidence, review and reporting workspace. Real-time agency integration and validated interception prediction are not established.
- **How do you demonstrate accuracy?** Define the target and coverage, test on later unseen outcomes, compare baselines, report errors and misses, and distinguish recorded-case performance from actual movement or interception performance.
- **Could it harm communities?** Biased observation and reporting can misdirect predictions. Limit detail and disclosure, audit coverage and errors, distinguish allegations from evidence, and require authorized human review. These measures reduce risk but do not guarantee fairness.
